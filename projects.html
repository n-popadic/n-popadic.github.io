LMU consulting project · with Center for Crisis Early Warning

Agentic RAG for conflict monitoring

A system that gathers information on armed conflicts from the internet, decides which sources to trust, and answers monitoring questions with cited evidence.

  • Designing the agentic RAG framework that crawls and reasons over news, structured conflict-event data and Wikipedia on recent conflicts.
  • Finding where reliable data on a conflict can be found at all, then filtering it by reliability, bias and duplication before anything reaches the model.
  • Answering questions with open-weights LLMs, so the system can be run and extended without paid APIs, with every answer citing the sources it came from.

Role

Research collaborator in an LMU consulting project

Biggest challenge so far

Finding quality data we are allowed to use. Many sites block crawlers in their robots.txt or forbid reuse in their terms of use, so every source has to be cleared before it goes in.

LMU research · for Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities

Aligning multilingual medieval surgical texts

The same medieval surgical treatises survive in French, Italian and Spanish versions. I build the pipeline that finds which passages correspond to each other.

  • Built an XML/TEI parser that handles complex editorial markup and abbreviation expansion, and turns the transcriptions into structured JSON.
  • Developing a cross-lingual alignment pipeline that matches the texts at chapter and sentence level with multilingual sentence embeddings.
  • Benchmarking several multilingual embedding models and alignment methods against ground-truth alignments.

Role

NLP research collaborator

Biggest challenge so far

Parsing the transcriptions into structured JSON. I do not speak French, Italian or Spanish, let alone their medieval versions, and every manuscript followed its own, inconsistent XML conventions, so I could not rely on AI without it overlooking things.

  • Python
  • XML/TEI
  • sentence embeddings
  • Hugging Face

Bachelor thesis · University of Novi Sad · grade 1.0

Junior swimmers under old and new age rules

The junior category for girls moved from ages 14–17 to 15–18. I tested which version tracks the boys' junior development (15–18) more closely.

  • Scraped race results from swimrankings.net and built a dataset of junior swimmers, three events per swimmer.
  • Cleaned missing-participation artifacts, checked normality with Shapiro–Wilk, and compared groups with t-tests, Mann–Whitney U, ANOVA and Tukey HSD.
  • Fitted linear regressions of progression across the junior years.

Result

Boys 15–18100%
Girls 14–17 (old)69%
Girls 15–18 (new)42%

Progression slope relative to the boys. The old category is the closer match.
