How the race works

The race is a small statistics project: real data, a cleaning step and a resampling model whose odds can be worked out exactly.

  1. Data collection

    I recorded 100 starts of my own with the same beep you hear here. Early presses were logged separately as false starts and not counted.

  2. Cleaning

    Reactions under 150 ms are anticipations, not reactions, so 2 were removed. Reactions over 450 ms were warm-up starts and misclicks, so 12 were removed. That left 86 clean starts: mean 280 ms, median 273 ms, standard deviation 40 ms.

  3. Sampling my ghost

    Each race, my ghost dives with one of my real starts, drawn at random from a pool. Your first race uses my fastest 20%. Every race you lose, the pool grows by a factor of 1.5, to 30%, 45% and 68%, so from your fifth race on all 86 starts are in play. It's best if people leave this site with a sense of victory. Once you beat me, it resets to my best.

  4. Your race

    Your reaction is measured live from the beep. After the dive we both swim at exactly my pace, so whoever reacts faster wins. Since people on phones react about 52 ms slower than on a keyboard (Pronk et al. 2023), phone users get a 0.05 s head start.

  5. The odds

    Your chance to win is simply the share of my pool that is slower than your start. At 250 ms you win 39% of first races. At 300 ms my best starts are out of reach until race 5, where you win 21% of the time, so about 76% of people beat me within ten races.
